03 Motivation

The Problem

Caring for Alzheimer's & elderly patients is a 24/7 job that no family can fully staff

①Memory loss

Patients struggle to recognize even close family and friends, causing distress and confusion.

②Missed medication

Forgotten or wrong doses are a leading cause of hospital readmission in dementia care.

③Unnoticed health events

Abnormal heart rate or distress can go undetected when no one is in the room.

④Caregiver burnout

Guardians cannot watch a patient every minute of every day.

04 Goals

Project Objectives

Build a low-cost, wearable assistant that needs zero manual operation
  • Recognize people automatically — identify family/friends by face and voice and remind the patient who they areRuns on a Raspberry Pi 5 built into a pair of smart glasses
  • Verify medication intake — recognize the medication box visually and confirm the dose was actually taken
  • Monitor vital signs continuously — stream live heart rate from a wearable strap straight into the patient's phone
  • Activate itself physically — turn the whole pipeline on the instant the glasses are worn, with no app, button, or caregiver action
  • Keep guardians informed in real time — push alerts the moment something needs attention

08 Module

Face & Voice Recognition

Helping the patient recognize the people in front of them
  • Multimodal matching — combines a face embedding (InsightFace) and a voice-print embedding (ECAPA-TDNN) for higher confidence than either alone
  • Enrollment — caregivers register a person's photo and a short voice sample once, from the mobile app's "Add Friend" screen
  • Storage — embeddings are stored as JSON arrays in the MongoDB people collection, linked to the patient and a relationship label (e.g. "Daughter")
  • Output — the glasses speak the result aloud through Piper TTS: "This is Sarah, your daughter."

10 Hardware Sensor ★ Key Innovation

LDR Light Sensor — Automatic Wear Detection

The glasses know when they're being worn — without a single button
💡
Sensor typeLight-Dependent Resistor (LDR), mounted on the glasses frame
ConnectionRaspberry Pi GPIO pin 17 (BCM numbering, digital input)
Signal logicHIGH (1) = light reaching sensor → glasses worn  ·  LOW (0) = blocked/dark → removed
DebounceRequires 3 consecutive HIGH reads (~0.9s) before activating — filters out false flickers
Poll rateRead every 0.3 seconds in a continuous background loop
Action takenAutomatically starts the face/voice recognition camera process when worn, stops it the moment it's removed

12 Core Idea ★ Key Innovation

Sensor-Triggered Automation on the Linux OS

The patient's physical action is the trigger — not a screen tap
  • No manual startup, ever. A background script (main_ai.py) is installed as a persistent process on the Pi's Linux OS — the same way the backend itself runs as a systemd service — so it is alive from the moment the device boots.
  • Idle state: while waiting, its only job is to watch the LDR sensor on GPIO 17.
  • The trigger: the instant the sensor reports "glasses on," the OS-level script spontaneously launches the entire AI pipeline — camera, face recognition, voice recognition. Taking the glasses off kills it again, just as automatically.
  • Same script, second job: a parallel medication-timing thread independently wakes the camera for MediScan exactly when a dose is due — regardless of whether the glasses were just put on.
  • Net effect: hardware sensor state drives software process lifecycle directly. The caregiver never opens an app, SSHes in, or flips a switch — the system runs itself.

13 Architecture

Mode-Switching: One Camera, Two Jobs

Face recognition and MediScan can never run at the same time — they share one camera

The constraint

The Pi has a single camera. Face/voice recognition and medication scanning are two separate processes that both need exclusive access to it.

The solution

A shared system_state document in MongoDB stores the current mode. The coordinator script polls it, kills the old process, and starts the right one.

  • Safety net 1: if a child process dies unexpectedly, it's auto-restarted on the next poll
  • Safety net 2: if MediScan runs longer than 5 minutes without confirming, the mode auto-reverts to face/voice recognition

19 Engineering

Challenges & Solutions

  • Camera contention between recognition and MediScan Solved with a single shared mode flag + coordinator process
  • Unreliable home Wi-Fi on the Pi Medication schedule cached locally so timing keeps working offline
  • False sensor triggers from light flicker 3-read debounce window on the LDR loop
  • BLE disconnects mid-monitoring Automatic reconnect logic with bounded retry/backoff

21 Wrap-up

Conclusion

  • Alzemora combines face/voice recognition, medication verification, and vital-sign monitoring into one wearable system
  • The standout design choice is physical, sensor-driven automation: the LDR sensor and a persistent Linux background script mean the system requires zero manual operation
  • Real-time alerts keep guardians and caregivers informed without needing to constantly check in
  • Built entirely on accessible, low-cost hardware — a Raspberry Pi 5, an LDR, and an off-the-shelf BLE heart-rate strap
